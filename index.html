About the Project

SAL is a growing, breathing archive of trees and the lives they hold.

A tree is never alone. Stand under any old tree and you are standing under a neighbourhood — birds in the canopy, bats at the fruit, caterpillars on the leaves, ferns and orchids on the bark, civets waiting below for what falls. SAL exists to tell these gathered stories together: the story of the tree, and the stories of everyone living on it.

What SAL does

  • Search a tree by any of its names — English, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Sinhala, scientific…
  • Explore the whole grove in alphabetical order.
  • Open a tree to read its scientific, cultural and historical stories.
  • Meet its residents: tap a species (or a marker on the illustrated tree) and the tree gently zooms into the part where that species lives, opening its own scientific, cultural and historical story.

How it feels

SAL's movement is inspired by livingbreathing.design — slow, organic, breathing transitions: pages rise like leaves, markers pulse like heartbeats, and the zoom into a tree part is one continuous, calm camera move rather than a jump.

Our first tree

The archive begins with the Fishtail Palm (Caryota urens), adapted from the beautiful Nature Classrooms poster (illustration & design credit: Nature Classrooms, CC BY-SA 4.0). Its residents — the Malabar grey hornbill, fruit bats, palm bob butterfly, epiphytes and the palm civet — are the first citizens of SAL.

The road ahead

More trees (the Sal itself is next!), more languages, more residents, field recordings of each tree's soundscape, and contributions from students, teachers and forest-dwellers. All content lives in one simple data block in the code (TREES), so adding a tree — or a real photograph — is just adding an entry.

The stories here are short, illustrative tellings meant for classrooms and curious minds; edit and deepen them freely.
