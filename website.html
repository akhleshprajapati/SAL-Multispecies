About the Project

SAL is a growing, breathing archive of trees and the lives they hold.

A tree is never alone. Stand under any old tree and you are standing under a neighbourhood — birds in the canopy, bats at the fruit, caterpillars on the leaves, ferns and orchids on the bark, civets waiting below for what falls. SAL tells these gathered stories together: the story of the tree, and the stories of everyone living on it.

What SAL does

  • Search a tree by any of its names — English, Hindi, Kannada, Malayalam, Sinhala, scientific…
  • Explore the whole grove in alphabetical order.
  • Open a tree to read its scientific, cultural and historical stories.
  • Meet its residents: tap a species (or a marker on the illustrated tree) and the tree gently zooms into the part where that species lives, opening its own scientific, cultural and historical story.

How SAL is built

The website machine lives in one file, website.html. Every tree lives in its own tiny data file inside the trees/ folder — for example trees/fishtail_palm.js or trees/sal.js — holding only its names, stories, residents and illustration. A tree may also supply a real photograph via artImg; the painted illustration remains as the offline fallback. Adding a tree means copying trees/_template.js, filling it in, and adding one line to website.html.

sal/
├── website.html         ← the whole website (engine + animations)
└── trees/
    ├── fishtail_palm.js ← one file per tree (pure data)
    ├── sal.js
    ├── _template.js     ← copy to grow the grove
    └── …

How it feels

SAL's movement is inspired by livingbreathing.design — slow, organic, breathing transitions: pages rise like leaves, markers pulse like heartbeats, and the zoom into a tree part is one continuous, calm camera move rather than a jump. Illustrations are painted with light: one sun direction, grainy bark, soft shadows and layered leaves.

Our first trees

The archive begins with the Fishtail Palm (Caryota urens) and the Sal (Shorea robusta), adapted from Nature Classrooms posters (CC BY-SA 4.0). Their residents — hornbills, fruit bats, palm bobs, epiphytes, civets, thrips, caterpillars, heartwood borers, forest birds and palm squirrels — are the first citizens of SAL.

The stories here are short, illustrative tellings meant for classrooms and curious minds; each tree file can be deepened freely.
